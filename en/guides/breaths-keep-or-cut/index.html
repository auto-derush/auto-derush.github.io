Breaths in editing: keep them or cut them?

2 min read

Between sentences, people breathe. In the edit, those breaths raise a real style question: natural, or maximum pace?

Why it's a style question

  • Keep breaths: the voice stays human and calm. Ideal for documentaries, interviews, podcasts.
  • Cut them: delivery becomes very dense, typical of fast-paced YouTube videos, Shorts and Reels. But cut too much and it sounds artificial.

Recognizing them

A breath is different from both silence and speech:

  • it's short (roughly 150 ms to 1 s);
  • it's quieter than the voice (at least 10 dB below);
  • it's airy: noise with no pitch, unlike a vowel.

By hand

On the waveform, a breath looks like a small bump between two blocks of speech. You can cut it, or lower its volume with keyframes to soften it without removing it.

With Auto-dérush

The Keep breaths checkbox decides:

  • checked: breaths between sentences are kept, only real silences are cut;
  • unchecked (default): they're cut like silences.

The panel uses the criteria above to tell a breath from a quiet syllable.

For a "YouTube" pace without sounding robotic, try the Tight preset with breaths unchecked, then listen: if the voice sounds out of breath, check the box and run it again.
