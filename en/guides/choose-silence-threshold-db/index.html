What dB threshold should you use to detect silences?

2 min read

Every silence-removal tool relies on a threshold: below this volume, sound is treated as silence. Set it wrong and it either clips words or lets silences through.

What the threshold measures

Volume is measured in decibels (dB), with 0 dB as the maximum. The more negative the number, the quieter the sound. In a typical voice recording:

  • the background noise of a quiet room is often between -60 and -50 dB;
  • the voice usually sits between -25 and -10 dB.

The threshold has to go between the two.

Too high, too low

  • Threshold too high (close to the voice, e.g. -20 dB): quiet syllables and sentence endings fall below it and get cut.
  • Threshold too low (close to the noise, e.g. -58 dB): background noise is mistaken for speech, and nothing gets cut.

The rule of thumb

Put the threshold closer to the noise than to the voice: about a third of the way up from the noise. With background noise at -55 dB and voice at -19 dB, the gap is 36 dB: a threshold around -43 dB works well.

That's exactly what Auto-dérush's Analyze selection button does: it measures the noise and voice of your clip, then suggests this threshold. One click on Apply sets it.

Reading the curve

After the analysis, Auto-dérush shows the clip's volume curve, with the threshold as a dashed line and the parts that will be cut in red. Clicking the curve moves Premiere's playhead there: press Space to listen and check.

When the contrast is too low

If voice and background noise are too close (wind, air conditioning, crowd), no threshold can separate them cleanly. The panel tells you. In that case, clean the audio first (noise reduction in Audition, or Enhance Speech), then run the analysis again.

The other settings that matter

  • Minimum silence length: a shorter pause is kept, even below the threshold.
  • Padding before and after: a little silence is kept around speech.
  • Minimum kept segment: an isolated click or cough shorter than this is removed.
