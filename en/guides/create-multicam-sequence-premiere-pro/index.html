Multicam Premiere Pro Tutorial: Create a Sequence Step by Step

3 min read

A multicam edit comes down to four moves: prepare the clips, build the source sequence, cut live, clean up. Here is the method, in order.

Before you start

Multicam only works well if the cameras recorded the same scene at the same time. Check three things:

  • Each camera has a clip (or a set of clips) in the project.
  • Every camera captured audio, even poor audio. It is your sync reference.
  • The cameras share the same frame rate, or close to it. Otherwise sync drifts over time.
Put the clips of one shoot in their own bin, and name them by camera: Cam A, Cam B, Cam C. It saves time in the dialog box.

1. Create the multi-camera source sequence

  1. In the Project panel, select all the clips from every camera.
  2. Right-click, then Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence.
  3. Name the sequence.
  4. Choose the Synchronize Point: Audio if every camera has sound, which is the usual case. Otherwise use Timecode, a marker, or the In Point.
  5. Click OK.

Premiere builds a source sequence with each camera on its own track, lined up on the audio. Open it and scrub through a few spots: lips should match the sound on every camera. If one camera is off, start again with another sync point. For more on fixing sync, see Sync Audio and Video in Premiere Pro Without a Clap.

2. Put the source sequence in a new sequence

  1. Create an empty sequence, or drag the source sequence onto the New Item icon.
  2. Place the source sequence in it. If the clip is not recognized as multicam, right-click it, go to Multi-Camera, and enable it.
  3. In the same menu, pick the starting camera.

Check which audio you are keeping. In the source sequence settings you chose what the sound follows: one camera, an external mic, or a mix. For an interview or a podcast, the external mic is nearly always the right choice.

3. Cut live

  1. In the Program Monitor, open the settings menu (the wrench) and choose Multi-Camera View. The cameras appear in a grid.
  2. Play the sequence, then click the camera you want, or press keys 1 to 9: the key matches the camera number.
  3. To record your choices, turn on the multi-camera record button before you press play. Each switch creates a cut in the timeline.

You can also cut without recording: place the playhead, then press the camera number, even while stopped.

Do one full pass without stopping, mistakes included. Fixing afterwards is faster than hunting for the perfect shot at every cut.

4. Fix and flatten

  • To change a camera afterwards, put the playhead on the clip and press the new camera number, or right-click the clip, go to Multi-Camera, and pick the camera.
  • To move a cut, use the Rolling Edit tool (N) or the Ripple Edit tool (B), as on any clip.
  • When the edit is done, right-click the clips, go to Multi-Camera, then Flatten. You get regular clips, which are easier to color and export.

Save a copy of the project before you flatten: it is not always easy to undo once you have made other changes.
