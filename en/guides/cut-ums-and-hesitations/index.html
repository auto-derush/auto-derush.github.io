How to cut "ums" and hesitations in Premiere Pro

2 min read

Once silences are gone, you're often left with "ums", "so…" and restarted sentences. They aren't handled the same way.

Three kinds of flaws

  • Silences: moments with no voice. They're detected by sound level, so automatically.
  • "Ums": voice, but no content. Sound level alone can't tell them apart from a word.
  • Flubbed sentences: the person starts over. Only listening (or a transcript) lets you pick the right take.

Step 1: remove silences first

Always start with silences: they're the slowest by hand and the easiest to automate. With Auto-dérush it takes one click. The clip is already much shorter, and only speech is left to listen to.

"Ums" are often surrounded by short pauses. With the Tight preset those pauses disappear, which makes the remaining "ums" less noticeable.

Step 2: "ums" with the transcript

Premiere Pro's text-based editing transcribes the sequence. In some languages it can flag filler words in the transcript: you delete them from the text and the cut is made on the timeline. Otherwise, search for "um" in the transcript and delete each occurrence.

Step 3: flubbed sentences by ear

Play the sequence at 1.5× (press L twice) and drop a marker (M) at each flub. Then go back to each marker and keep the best take.

What Auto-dérush does, and doesn't

Auto-dérush cuts silences, breaths (optional) and crew voices. It doesn't detect "ums" or flubbed sentences: that would require understanding what's said. Combining it with text-based editing gives you the fastest workflow.
