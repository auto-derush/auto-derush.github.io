How to edit an interview fast in Premiere Pro

2 min read

An interview often means an hour of footage for ten useful minutes. Here's a simple method to get to a clean first cut quickly.

Step 1: prepare the sequence

  • Put the camera video and the lavalier audio on the timeline.
  • Sync them (right-click > Synchronize, using the audio waveform), then link them.
  • Remove the obvious junk first: setup, "let's do that again".

At this point the sequence only contains footage: no music or graphics yet. That's the right time to cut the silences.

Step 2: cut the silences

This is the slowest step by hand: each pause takes two cuts and a ripple delete. With Auto-dérush, select the clips (video + mic) and click Cut silences: the silences are cut on every track at once, and the video stays in sync with the mic.

Tip: click Analyze selection first. The panel measures the room's background noise and the voice level, then suggests a threshold that fits your recording.

Step 3: remove the questions and crew voices

If the interviewer is off camera, their voice is faint in the guest's lavalier mic but present in the camera audio. The Cut other voices option compares both tracks: when a voice is louder elsewhere than in the reference mic, it's someone else, and that part is cut.

Step 4: refine the pacing

The automatic cut gives you a clean base. What's left is editorial work: picking the best answers, reordering, adding B-roll to hide jump cuts. With the silences already gone, this goes much faster because you only listen to speech.

How much time do you save?

By hand, cleaning 10 minutes of interview often takes 20 to 40 minutes. With an automatic cut, the cutting itself takes under 30 seconds: you only need to check it and make the editorial calls.
