How to edit a multicam video podcast in Premiere Pro, automatically

2 min read

A podcast shot with several cameras means hours of editing: cutting silences, then picking the right camera for every sentence. Both can be automated.

The classic workflow

Premiere Pro has native multi-camera editing: you create a multi-camera source sequence, then switch angles live during playback. It works, but you have to watch everything in real time, and cutting silences remains a separate step.

The idea: each mic tells you who's talking

In a podcast, each speaker has their own mic. At any moment, the loudest mic tells you who is talking. So analyzing the mics is enough to know which camera to show.

With Auto-dérush

  1. Put the cameras on video tracks (V1, V2, V3…) and the mics on audio tracks, all synced.
  2. Check Multicam edit (podcast) in the panel.
  3. Give each track a role: "Speaker 1", "Speaker 2", "Wide shot" for video, "Speaker 1 mic", "Speaker 2 mic" for audio.
  4. Select all the clips and click Cut silences.

The panel first cuts the silences (when nobody is talking), then splits the sequence at every change of speaker and enables the camera of whoever is talking. The sound is never cut.

Settings that make the edit feel natural

  • Minimum shot length (2 s by default): a "yes" or a "hmm" doesn't switch cameras.
  • Wide shot when several people talk: if two mics are within 6 dB of each other, the wide shot is shown.
  • Wide shot after X seconds: for variety and reactions when staying on the same person for a long time.
  • Wide shot on big cuts: hides the jump cut when a long silence was removed mid-sentence.
This isn't a native multicam sequence: each shot is a piece of clip on its own track, with the other cameras disabled. So you can touch up any shot by hand afterwards.

The result

A complete first cut, silences removed and cameras chosen, in seconds. You refine a few shots by hand instead of editing everything.
