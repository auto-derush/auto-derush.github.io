How to Edit a Two-Camera Interview in Premiere Pro, Fast

3 min read

Two cameras, two mics, an hour of footage: the edit comes down to doing things in the right order. Here is the method that keeps you from doing the work twice.

Prepare the footage before opening the timeline

Sort your files by camera: one folder for Cam A, one for Cam B. Keep the names clear. You will see them again inside the sequence.

Check one thing first: did each camera record audio, even rough audio? That audio is what you sync on. If a camera recorded none, you need a clap or a visual cue.

Tip: keep each speaker's external mic on its own track. You can then lower the one who is not talking without touching the other.

Step 1: create the multi-camera source sequence

  1. In the Project panel, select the clips from both cameras (and the external audio files, if any).
  2. Right-click, then Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence.
  3. In the dialog, set Synchronize Point to Audio. Premiere aligns the clips on the waveform.
  4. If you have an external mic, pick it as the sequence audio instead of the camera audio.
  5. Click OK. A source sequence appears in the project.

Drop it into a new sequence. Listen to the start, the middle and the end. Sync drift shows up mostly on long takes.

Step 2: switch between cameras

Turn on the multi-camera view in the Program Monitor. You now see both angles at once.

  1. Turn on recording of camera switches.
  2. Press play.
  3. Press 1 or 2 (the camera number) when you want to change angle.

Premiere cuts the clip at each key press. You can also park the playhead on an exact frame, cut with Ctrl+K, then change the angle of the cut section.

Simple shot/reverse-shot rules:

  • Show the person talking, unless a reaction matters.
  • Switch on a breath, the end of a sentence or a change of topic, never mid-word.
  • Do not cut every two seconds. A shot held for 5 to 10 seconds reads better.
  • Keep a wide shot for the moments when both people talk at once.

Step 3: lock the edit, then clean up

Once the angles work, right-click the multi-camera clip and choose Flatten. Each shot becomes a normal clip. You can then adjust framing, color or length shot by shot.

This is when you cut the pauses. The trap: deleting a gap on one track only pushes everything else out of place. The guide on rippling across multiple tracks without losing sync shows how to keep video and mics aligned.

Watch for jump cuts. If you remove a pause in the middle of a shot, the picture jumps. Cut where the angle already changes whenever you can.

Checks before export

  • Listen to every shot change: the audio must not jump.
  • Look at the wide shots: are both people framed properly?
  • Check that music tracks have not shifted.
  • Play the whole sequence once at normal speed.
