How to cut a video with an external mic without losing sync

2 min read

Camera audio is rarely good, so voice gets recorded on a lavalier or a separate recorder. The challenge is keeping both aligned through the whole edit.

1. Sync at the start

  • Put the video and the mic file one above the other.
  • Select both, right-click > Synchronize > Audio. Premiere lines up the waveforms.
  • Cut or mute the camera's scratch audio if you're not using it.
  • Right-click > Link: video and mic now move together.

2. Cut without drifting

Drift happens when you cut one track and not the other. To avoid it:

  • cut with Ctrl + Shift + K (all tracks);
  • ripple delete pieces that contain both the video and the audio;
  • avoid clips overlapping on other tracks during the rough cut.

3. Let automation do it

With Auto-dérush, select the video and the mic, then click Cut silences. Every selected track is cut on the same frames, then moved together. Sync doesn't budge, even with 200 cuts.

Which track is used for detection?

By default, the panel listens to the mix of unmuted tracks, that is, what you hear. You can also pick a specific reference track (A1, A2…), even if it's muted: handy for detecting on the lavalier while also cutting the video and scratch audio.

If every selected audio track is muted, nothing is cut and the panel tells you: you can't detect silences in sound you can't hear.

4. Check

After the cut, play a few random passages while watching the lips. If sync was right before, it's right after: cuts land on whole frames.
