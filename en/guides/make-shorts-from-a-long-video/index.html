How to make Shorts and Reels from a long video

2 min read

An interview or a podcast often holds several moments that would make good short-form clips. Pulling them out efficiently takes a method.

1. Find the moments

While playing, drop a marker (M) at each strong moment: a punchy line, an anecdote, a surprising number. A good Short holds one idea, 20 to 60 seconds long.

2. One sequence per clip

Select the passage with In and Out points (I and O), then create a new sequence from the selection. Keep one sequence per Short: it's easier to reframe and export.

3. Tighten the pace

Short-form content forgives no dead air. Run Auto-dérush on the clip with the Tight preset and breaths unchecked: silences cut from 300 ms, short padding.

4. Go vertical

Use Sequence > Auto Reframe Sequence to switch to 9:16: Premiere tracks the subject in the frame. Then check the shots where the framing hesitates.

5. Captions and hook

  • Big, readable captions in the middle of the screen.
  • A written hook in the first 2 seconds.
  • No logo or text at the bottom of the screen, where app interfaces cover it.
Do the rough cut before reframing: there's less footage to reframe, and cuts stay in sync with the audio.
