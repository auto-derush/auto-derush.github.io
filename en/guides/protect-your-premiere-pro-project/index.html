How to protect your Premiere Pro project before a big change

2 min read

A rough cut you don't like, a crash, a wrong move… A few habits are enough to never lose your work.

1. Auto-save

In Preferences > Auto Save, save every 5 to 10 minutes and keep several versions. Copies go into the project's "Adobe Premiere Pro Auto-Save" folder.

2. Duplicate the sequence

Before a major change (rough cut, reframe, restructuring), right-click > Duplicate the sequence in the Project panel. Rename the copy with the date or the stage.

3. Save as a new version

Ctrl + Shift + S (Save As) with a version number: "Project_v03". Simple and effective.

4. What Auto-dérush does

Auto-dérush has this safety net built in:

  • before every cut, a copy of the sequence is created in the project, named with the time (for example "Interview (before cut 14:32)");
  • the Undo cut button reopens that copy under the original name.
Why not just Ctrl + Z? A rough cut makes hundreds of edits, and Premiere doesn't let extensions group them into a single undo. A sequence copy is safer.

5. Back up your footage

The project doesn't contain the videos: keep a copy of your footage on another drive, at least until delivery.
