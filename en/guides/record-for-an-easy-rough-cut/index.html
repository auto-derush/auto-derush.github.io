How to record your voice for an easy rough cut

2 min read

A good rough cut starts at the shoot. The more the voice stands out from background noise, the easier silences are to find and cut cleanly.

Voice-to-noise contrast is everything

All silence detection compares the voice with the background noise. A gap of 30 dB or more makes cutting very precise. Under 15 dB, voice and noise blur together, and no tool can cut cleanly.

The mic

  • Lavalier or a dynamic mic close to the mouth: the voice is loud, room noise stays low.
  • Avoid the camera mic on its own: it picks up as much room as voice.
  • With several speakers, one mic per person: that's also what lets a podcast edit automatically pick the camera of whoever is talking.

The room

  • Turn off air conditioning, fans, the fridge if you can.
  • A furnished room (curtains, sofa, bookshelves) echoes less than an empty one.
  • Close windows facing the street.

Recording level

Aim for a voice around -18 to -12 dB on the recorder, with peaks that never hit 0 dB. Too low and the voice gets close to the noise; too high and it clips.

Before the rough cut

  • If the audio is noisy, apply noise reduction before detecting silences.
  • In Auto-dérush, click Analyze selection: the panel shows the measured background noise and voice. If the gap is too small, it tells you instead of cutting at random.
Bonus: let 5 seconds of silence roll at the start of each take. It's a reference for the background noise, and for noise reduction.
