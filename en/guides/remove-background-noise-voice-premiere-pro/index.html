How to Remove Background Noise From a Voice in Premiere Pro

3 min read

Hiss, fan hum, air conditioning: background noise can be fixed in a few minutes in Premiere Pro. The trick is not to damage the voice by cleaning too hard.

Before you touch any setting

Listen to the clip on headphones. Work out what kind of noise you have, because each one needs a different fix:

  • Hiss (mic, camera, preamp): steady and high-pitched.
  • Rumble (fan, air conditioning, traffic): steady and low.
  • Room echo: the voice sounds hollow. Strictly speaking, that is not noise.

Steady noise cleans up well. Irregular noise (voices in the background, a door slamming) does not. Cut those out instead.

Method 1: the Essential Sound panel

This is the fastest way.

  1. Open Window > Essential Sound.
  2. Select your audio clip in the timeline.
  3. Click Dialogue to set the clip type.
  4. Tick Reduce Noise and set the slider. Start low, around 3 to 5, and raise it in small steps.
  5. If needed, also tick Reduce Rumble for low frequencies, and DeReverb if the room rings.

Listen after every change. If the voice turns metallic or sounds underwater, you have gone too far. Back off.

If your version of Premiere Pro has Enhance Speech in the Dialogue section, try it on very noisy recordings. Adjust the mix slider to keep the result natural. It works well, but it changes the voice more than plain noise reduction does.

Method 2: the DeNoise effect

For finer control, use an audio effect.

  1. In the Effects panel, search for DeNoise.
  2. Drag it onto the clip.
  3. In Effect Controls, adjust Amount. A moderate value is almost always enough.

You can also add a Parametric Equalizer and cut everything below roughly 80 Hz. A voice has nothing useful down there, and this removes much of the rumble.

Tip: toggle the effect on and off while you listen. Your ear gets used to the processed sound quickly, and you end up overdoing it.

Applying the setting to every clip

If you have already cut your sequence, you have dozens of small clips. Do not repeat the setting by hand:

  1. Set up one clip.
  2. Copy it (Ctrl+C).
  3. Select the other audio clips from the same source.
  4. Use Edit > Paste Attributes and keep the audio effects.

When the noise stays anyway

Cleanup has limits. If the noise is as loud as the voice, no setting will save the recording. Fix it at the source: mic closer to the mouth, quieter room, fan off. How to improve interview audio in Premiere Pro covers the rest of the audio chain.

What to remember

  • Identify the type of noise before adjusting anything.
  • Essential Sound for speed, DeNoise for fine tuning.
  • Raise sliders in small steps and listen on headphones.
  • Noise that loud is a recording problem, not an editing one.
