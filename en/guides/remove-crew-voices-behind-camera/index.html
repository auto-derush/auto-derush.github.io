Automatically cut crew voices from behind the camera

2 min read

In interviews or field shoots, you often hear the crew: an off-camera question, a "that's good" from the camera operator. Removing them by hand means listening to everything.

The principle: compare two mics

The speaker wears a lavalier: their voice is loud and close in it. The crew is far from that mic but well picked up by the camera mic. Comparing the two tracks tells you who is talking:

  • loud voice in the lavalier → it's the speaker, keep it;
  • voice louder in the camera mic → it's someone else, cut it.

The manual method

Listen to the whole clip, find every crew interjection, cut at the start and end, then ripple delete. On a long interview, it's tedious and easy to miss some.

With Auto-dérush

  1. Choose the lavalier as the reference track.
  2. Check Cut other voices.
  3. Also select the ambient track (the camera audio, for example).
  4. Click Analyze selection: passages detected as "other voice" show up in orange on the curve.
  5. Click Cut silences.

The panel smooths the difference between the mics over 300 ms, then automatically splits it into two groups: the speaker and everyone else. "Other voice" moments are cut like silences.

Adjusting sensitivity

  • Some of the speaker's sentences get cut? Move Other-voice sensitivity to the left.
  • Crew voices remain? Move it to the right.
If both mics pick up the same thing (less than 6 dB apart on average), the panel cuts nothing more and tells you: the separation wouldn't be reliable.
