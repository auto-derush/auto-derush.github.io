How to remove silences in Premiere Pro: the manual way and the automatic way

2 min read

Silences are the gaps between sentences: hesitations, long breaths, moments spent looking for words. Cutting them makes a video feel alive, but doing it by hand is the slowest part of editing.

Why cut silences?

On YouTube, in courses or in podcasts, viewers drop off as soon as the pace slows. A raw 10-minute interview often contains 2 to 3 minutes of silence. Removing it gives you a shorter, denser video that's nicer to watch.

The manual method in Premiere Pro

  1. Make the audio track taller so you can see the waveform (double-click the track header, or Alt + scroll).
  2. Look for flat areas: those are the silences.
  3. Put the playhead at the start of a silence and cut with Ctrl + K (Add Edit), or Ctrl + Shift + K to cut every track at once.
  4. Do the same at the end of the silence.
  5. Select the cut piece, then right-click > Ripple Delete (or Shift + Delete) to close the gap.
  6. Repeat… for every silence.

On a 10-minute clip, that's easily 100 to 200 cuts. It's precise, but slow, and mistakes creep in: a clipped word, or an external mic drifting out of sync with the video.

Tip: always listen to the transition after a cut. A silence trimmed too tight makes the voice sound like it "jumps".

The automatic method with Auto-dérush

Auto-dérush is a Premiere Pro panel that does the job for you:

  1. Select your clips in the timeline (video alone, or video and mic).
  2. Pick a preset: Tight, Natural or Loose.
  3. Click Cut silences.

The panel analyzes the audio, finds every silence, cuts frame-accurately on all selected tracks at once, then closes the gaps. A 10-minute interview is processed in under 30 seconds.

What keeps words from being clipped

  • Padding: a little time is kept before speech resumes (100 ms by default) and after a sentence ends (150 ms).
  • Minimum silence length: short pauses (under 500 ms with the Natural preset) are kept, so the rhythm stays natural.
  • Two-level detection: speech starts at the chosen threshold but only stops 6 dB lower, so quieter word endings aren't cut.

What if you don't like the result?

Before every cut, a backup copy of the sequence is created in the project. The Undo cut button reopens it in one click.

In short

The manual method is still handy for one or two precise cuts. For a whole clip, the automatic way saves dozens of minutes per video, with no risk of knocking the audio out of sync.
