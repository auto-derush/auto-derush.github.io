Ripple delete across multiple tracks without knocking audio out of sync

2 min read

You cut a silence, ripple delete it… and the mic no longer lines up with the video. It's one of the most common traps when editing with external audio.

Where does the offset come from?

Ripple Delete (Shift + Delete) closes a gap by shifting everything after it. But it only shifts tracks that have no clip at that spot, and it follows track targeting. If the video and the mic aren't cut at exactly the same place, or if a track is locked, Premiere refuses, or shifts one track and not the other.

Good habits when cutting by hand

  • Cut every track at once with Ctrl + Shift + K instead of track by track.
  • Link the video and its synced audio (right-click > Link) so they stay together.
  • Check that no clip on another track (music, for example) overlaps the silence: it blocks the ripple.
  • Work on a sequence that only contains footage: add music and graphics after the rough cut.

What Auto-dérush does

Auto-dérush was built around this problem:

  • cuts land exactly on a frame of the sequence;
  • each silence edge is cut on all tracks at once, like Ctrl + Shift + K, which keeps video linked to its audio;
  • selected clips are moved one by one, earliest to latest, for a reliable result even with many cuts;
  • unselected clips placed after the selection are shifted, just like Premiere's Ripple Delete.
If a track holds an unselected clip that overlaps a silence (music, for example), it isn't shifted, and the panel tells you which one. You stay in control.

With an external mic

This is the classic case: the camera records picture and scratch audio, the lavalier records the voice. Select both the video and the mic before running the cut: both are cut at the same spot, and sync stays perfect.
