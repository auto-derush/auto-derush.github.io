Sync Audio and Video in Premiere Pro Without a Clap

3 min read

No clap and no shared timecode? Premiere Pro can still line up your cameras and mic by comparing their waveforms. Here are the three methods, and how to stop a later cut from breaking the sync.

The idea: Premiere matches the waveforms

Every camera records sound, even if it is poor. Your external mic records the same sound, much cleaner. Premiere finds the matching waveform shapes in both files and shifts the clips until they line up.

So each camera must have picked up the sound of the scene. A camera with its mic off cannot be synced this way.

Method 1: merge one video clip and the mic

The cleanest option for one camera and one mic.

  1. In the Project panel, select the video clip and the mic's audio file.
  2. Right-click, Merge Clips.
  3. Under Synchronize Point, choose Audio.
  4. Tick Remove Audio from AV clip if you do not want the camera sound. Leave it unticked to keep it as a reference track.
  5. Click OK. Premiere creates a new clip with the camera picture and the mic sound aligned.

Use that merged clip in your timeline instead of the two originals.

Method 2: sync in the timeline

Handy when the clips are already placed.

  1. Put the video on V1 and the mic on A2, roughly lined up.
  2. Select both clips.
  3. Right-click, Synchronize, then choose Audio.
  4. Premiere moves one clip to match the other.

This method moves clips. Check afterwards that nothing was overwritten.

Method 3: several cameras, one multi-camera source sequence

For two or more cameras plus a mic.

  1. Select all the cameras and the mic in the Project panel.
  2. Right-click, Create Multi-Camera Source Sequence.
  3. Under Synchronize Point, choose Audio.
  4. Pick the audio track to use, usually the external mic.
  5. Click OK, drop the source sequence into a new sequence, and turn on the multi-camera view to switch angles.
Tip: rename your files before syncing (CAM1, CAM2, MIC). When a camera drifts, you will know which one.

Check the sync before you edit

Automatic sync can get it wrong, especially with noise or an echoey room.

  • Listen to a spot near the start and another near the end. Drift shows up mostly on long takes.
  • Zoom into the waveforms and compare camera and mic: the peaks should land together.
  • Watch the lips on a "p" or a "b". One or two frames of offset is easy to see.
  • If Premiere fails, try a shorter, sharper passage: a hand clap, a loud word.

When sync breaks after the fact

The real danger comes during the edit. Cut one track without the others and everything slides. Two rules:

  • Select all linked tracks before you ripple delete.
  • Lock any track that must not move, such as music.

The details are in Ripple delete across multiple tracks.

Common mistakes

  • Clips that start at different times: if the external mic started after the camera, Premiere will place the clip later. That is normal, not an error.
  • Unusable camera audio: too much wind or a muffled mic. Clap your hands at the start next time, even if it is not required.
  • Mixed frame rates: cameras at 25 and 30 fps can drift on long takes. Shoot at the same frame rate.
  • Camera stops: if a camera stops and restarts, sync each piece separately.
